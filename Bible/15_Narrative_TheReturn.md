# The Return — Core Arc
### "The hunt for a way home"

## Premise
A paleontologist, alone, mid-excavation, is thrown backward into the
Cretaceous with no warning and no mechanism he understands. There is no one
else here. No camp of survivors, no tribe, no rescue team. His training —
species identification, geology, tool-use archaeology — is the only asset
that transfers.

## The First Gem
Early in the game, in the excavation site itself (the ground he was standing
on when the transition happened), he finds a fragment of crystalline material
that does not belong to the surrounding rock layer. It is out of place
geologically — the kind of anomaly a scientist is trained to flag and can't
ignore. It doesn't behave like anything he can test with what he's carrying.

His hypothesis, stated in his own field notes, not by any narrator: something
placed it there, or something like it caused what happened to him. If there's
a pattern, there should be a second one — and geological anomalies of this
kind tend to cluster by rock-layer exposure, which points toward colder,
higher terrain far from the temperate zone he woke up in.

## The Second Gem — Snowy Rockside
The stated goal of the entire playthrough: reach the Snowy Rockside biome and
find the second fragment. This is not a quest handed to him — it's a
conclusion he draws himself from evidence, stated once in field notes and
referenced sparingly afterward so it doesn't become a nagging objective
marker. The player pursues it because survival mechanics (resource scarcity,
territorial danger, weather) make the terrain toward Snowy Rockside genuinely
harder, and each step closer is a real cost, not a waypoint.

## Structural notes for other agents
- **No exposition dump.** The backstory above is revealed only through field
  notes (`Narrative_FieldNote_*` tags) discovered in the world, in the order
  the player finds them — never a cutscene, never a loading-screen text wall.
- **No NPC delivers this information.** Ever. If a future agent is tempted to
  add a guide, elder, or messenger to explain the gem, that violates the
  single-survivor premise and should be rejected.
- **The arc is proof of competence, not awakening.** He does not become
  "enlightened" or "connected" to the world. He becomes better at surviving
  it — faster identification, better tools, fewer near-misses, and eventually
  the ability to get large herbivores to tolerate his presence (domestication,
  owned by Agent #11/#14 mechanically, referenced narratively here only as
  "the animal remembers you").
- **Ending condition:** reaching and recovering the second gem in Snowy
  Rockside is the win-condition anchor. What happens narratively at that
  point (does he get home, does it fail, is it ambiguous) is intentionally
  left open pending Miguel's direction — flagging this as the one open
  narrative decision that needs human input before final cutscene/ending
  content is authored.
