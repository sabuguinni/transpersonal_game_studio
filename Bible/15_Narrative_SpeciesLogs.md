# Species Logs — Field Identification Cards
### Written in-character by the protagonist (paleontologist), for the 5 species present in the playable world.

Format matches what a field scientist actually carries: diet, size class, danger
rating, and approach signals — not lore flavor text. This is reference material
he uses to decide whether to run, hide, or keep observing.

---

## TRICERATOPS
- **Diet:** Herbivore — low ferns, cycads, tough fibrous plants (beak built for shearing).
- **Size:** ~8-9m adult, ~9-12 tonnes. Subadults smaller, seen 4-5m.
- **Danger:** Low unless cornered or defending young. Frill + horns are a real threat
  if it charges — it will charge, low and fast, if pushed.
- **Approach signal:** Head lowered + frill angled toward you = warning. Back away
  laterally, never turn your back and run in a straight line.
- **Field note:** Herds observed 5-15 strong. Treat any lone adult near a herd as
  a sentry — it is not actually alone.

## PARASAUROLOPHUS
- **Diet:** Herbivore — conifer needles, higher browse than Triceratops takes.
- **Size:** ~9-10m, lightly built, long hollow cranial crest.
- **Danger:** Negligible directly. The crest is a resonance chamber — the call
  carries far and is often the first warning of a predator elsewhere, not a threat
  from the animal itself.
- **Approach signal:** None needed for the animal — but a change in ITS calling
  pattern (frequency spike, then silence) means something is hunting nearby.
  Listen to them as an alarm system.
- **Field note:** Herd animal, same caution as Triceratops regarding groups.

## ANKYLOSAURUS
- **Diet:** Herbivore — low ground cover, ferns, close to the earth.
- **Size:** ~6-7m, heavily armored, tail ends in a bone club.
- **Danger:** Low if left alone. The club can break bone — do not stand behind
  or beside the tail's swing radius (roughly one body length) if it feels cornered.
- **Approach signal:** Slow, unbothered grazing = safe to observe from a
  distance. It rarely flees; it just doesn't fight unless struck first.
- **Field note:** Best animal in this world to practice patience around. Good
  baseline for calibrating what "not a threat" looks like before facing
  something that is.

## VELOCIRAPTOR
- **Diet:** Carnivore — opportunistic, pack-coordinated when hunting larger prey,
  solitary scavenging otherwise.
- **Size:** ~1.5-2m long, hip height roughly waist-high on a person. Small does
  not mean safe.
- **Danger:** High. Flanking, patient, tests before committing. A single
  raptor circling is often a scout for more.
- **Approach signal:** Head-low stalking gait, tail rigid = hunting posture.
  If it circles rather than charging, it hasn't decided you're worth the risk
  yet — do not run, back toward a wall or rock face, make yourself a bad bet.
- **Field note:** The only species in this world I actively avoid studying up
  close. Everything I know about it, I know from a safe distance.

## TSINTAOSAURUS
- **Diet:** Herbivore — mid-height browse, distinctive forward crest used for
  display and possibly resonance like Parasaurolophus.
- **Size:** ~10m, bipedal/quadrupedal capable, tall unicorn-like crest.
- **Danger:** Low. No offensive anatomy beyond size and a defensive kick if
  truly cornered.
- **Approach signal:** Crest angle changes with alertness — upright and rigid
  means it has noticed you. Otherwise unbothered by observation at range.
- **Field note:** Least data of the five so far. Fewer individuals encountered.
  Worth more observation time if I get the chance.
