# Field Notes — Solo Survivor's Log
### Narrative & Dialogue Agent #15 — Cycle PROD_CYCLE_AUTO_20260817_001

The protagonist is alone. No tribe, no camp, no other humans in this world. He is
a paleontologist thrown backward into the Cretaceous, and the only voice in this
game is his — spoken aloud to no one, written down because writing is a habit
that keeps a scientist's mind from breaking.

These are not "notes for the player." They are entries he would actually make:
species observed, terrain read, a body count of near-misses. Cold, precise,
occasionally cracking.

---

## ENTRY 001 — Day 1
*No location fix yet. Ground is wrong — red laterite where there should be
temperate clay. Sun angle is wrong too. I am not where I was.*

I have three matches, a rock hammer, and a note-pad that used to be for field
observations of a dig site that — as far as I can tell — does not exist yet.
I am going to keep using it for the same purpose. Observation is the only tool
I have that still works exactly as intended.

## ENTRY 002 — First large animal, Day 1
*Triceratops, subadult, ~4m. Grazing, not aggressive. Frill has old scarring —
predator strike, healed.*

It didn't charge. It watched me for maybe eight seconds, decided I wasn't worth
the calories, and went back to the ferns. Good. I'd like more encounters to end
exactly like that.

## ENTRY 003 — Sound at dusk, Day 2
*Low resonant call, repeated three times, ~40m north. Consistent with hollow
cranial crest — Parasaurolophus. No visual confirmation.*

I know what that sound is. I still didn't sleep.

## ENTRY 004 — Near-miss, Day 4
*Velociraptor, single individual, flanking behavior. It circled me twice before
I saw it. That is the part that should scare me more than it does.*

Talked myself through it out loud — "it's testing you, it's not committed,
back to the rock face, don't run" — because there was nobody else to say it to
me. Worked. I'm alive to write "worked."

## ENTRY 005 — Ankylosaurus, Day 6
*Armored quadruped, tail club, slow. No interest in me whatsoever. Grazing
posture identical for eleven minutes of observation.*

This is the first animal here I've felt something like affection for. It is a
tank that eats plants and wants nothing from me. I'll take it.

## ENTRY 006 — Storm, Day 9
*Barometric drop before visible cloud. Herd animals moved to low ground almost
an hour before the first rain. Note: watch the herds, not the sky.*

## ENTRY 012 — On the gem, Day 20
*The fragment reacts to nothing I can test with what I have. No warmth, no
mass anomaly I can measure by hand. But it wasn't here when the ground was
here — it's foreign to this rock layer. Which means someone or something
placed it, or it fell the same way I did.*

If there's a way back, it isn't behind me. It's further in. Colder ground,
higher altitude — that's where the second one has to be, if the pattern from
the first location holds. I'm calling it the Snowy Rockside for now, until I
have a real name for it.
