# Species Field Guide — Protagonist's Notebook

First-person species entries, written as the paleontologist encounters and
studies each animal. Scientifically grounded (diet, build, threat signature,
approach cues) — this is survival information, not lore flavor text. Covers the
five species confirmed present in the live world (Dino_ actors, verified this
cycle: 40 total, unchanged).

---

## Triceratops
**Classification:** Ceratopsian, herbivore, herd animal.
**Build:** Quadrupedal, ~8-9m, triple horn arrangement (two brow horns, one
nasal), solid frill.
**Diet:** Low browse — ferns, cycads, tough understory vegetation. The frill
and beak suggest a shearing bite adapted to fibrous plant matter.
**Threat signature:** Low unless cornered or defending young. A lowered head
with the frill angled forward is a charge warning — the horns are a real
goring risk at close range.
**Field note:**
> "They graze in loose herds and barely lift their heads when I pass at
> distance. But I watched one square up on a Velociraptor testing the herd's
> edge — the charge covered fifteen meters in under two seconds. Respect the
> distance, always the distance."

## Parasaurolophus
**Classification:** Hadrosaur, herbivore, herd animal.
**Build:** Bipedal/quadrupedal, ~10m, distinctive backward-curving cranial
crest (hollow — almost certainly a resonating chamber).
**Diet:** Mixed browse and graze, wide grinding dental battery — conifer
needles, angiosperm leaves, soft vegetation.
**Threat signature:** Minimal directly — no weapons to speak of. The real
danger is what they attract: their alarm call carries a long way and travels
faster than the animal that triggered it.
**Field note:**
> "The crest call is unmistakable once you've heard it — low, hollow, carries
> across the whole valley. When I hear it I don't ask why. I get to cover.
> Whatever set them off is already closer than the sound suggests."

## Ankylosaurus
**Classification:** Ankylosaurid, herbivore, armored, largely solitary.
**Build:** Low, wide, heavily armored quadruped, osteoderm plating across the
back and flanks, terminal tail club.
**Diet:** Ground-level ferns and low shrubs — the low center of gravity and
wide gut cavity point to a slow-fermentation, high-bulk diet.
**Threat signature:** Low to moving predators from the front (nearly
impenetrable), genuinely dangerous from any angle if the tail is engaged —
the club can break bone.
**Field note:**
> "Nothing about the front half of this animal is worth attacking. I found
> drag marks and a broken sapling near one — the club connected with something
> that didn't walk away clean. Approach from the flank if at all, and never
> stand behind it."

## Velociraptor
**Classification:** Dromaeosaurid, small predator, pack hunter.
**Build:** ~2m, bipedal, feathered, sickle claw on the second toe of each
foot, lightly built for speed and coordinated pursuit.
**Diet:** Active predation — small to mid-sized vertebrates, opportunistic on
carrion.
**Threat signature:** High in numbers, moderate alone. The real danger is
never the one you see — it's the two or three you don't, positioning while
the first one holds your attention.
**Field note:**
> "If I see one, I stop and count before I move again. They don't rush in
> alone — the one in view is a decoy as often as not. I've started walking
> with my back to rock faces whenever I hear that clicking call repeat from
> more than one direction."

## Tsintaosaurus
**Classification:** Hadrosaur, herbivore, herd/mixed-herd animal.
**Build:** Bipedal-capable hadrosaur, single unicorn-like forward crest
projection, broad duck-bill.
**Diet:** Browse-dominant — conifers, cycads, tough seasonal vegetation,
similar dental battery to other hadrosaurs.
**Threat signature:** Minimal. Skittish rather than aggressive — first
response to disturbance is flight, not confrontation.
**Field note:**
> "Easiest animal on this ridge to misjudge as harmless and easiest to lose
> as a food source if I spook it — it bolts at the first wrong footstep and
> doesn't stop for a long time. Slow approach, always downwind."
